WHAT WENT WRONG WITH INDONESIA'S FIGHT AGAINST CORRUPTION?
DROP YOUR THOUGHTS IN THE COMMENTS
In 2005,
Indonesia lost 8.84 trillion
rupiah across 40 major
corruption cases.
A figure that shocked
the country at the
time.
Today a single scandal
can wipe out 20
times that amount.
We went through all
40 major court
verdicts and cases from
2005 and compared them
to the scandals unfolding
this year.
And the comparisons show
how much bigger and
deeper the theft has
grown
over the last two
decades.
2005 was the first
year sitting regional leaders,
ministers and election
officials landed in the
new anti-corruption court.
The case that kicked
it off involved A.P.,
then governor of Nanggroe
Aceh Darussalam,
who was convicted of
marking up the purchase
of an MI-2 helicopter
by 13.6 billion rupiah.
In April 2005,
judges sentenced him to
10 years of prison.
Around the same time,
collective budget skimming
inside local parliaments
was breaking out across
the country.
In Padang City,
27 DPRD members got
four year sentences for
misusing,
10.4 billion rupiah from
the regional budget.
In West Sumatra Province,
43 DPRD members were
implicated in a 6.4
billion rupiah budget case.
From Semarang,
Cirebon,
Solo,
Manado,
and Donggala,
local council members were
carving up regional budgets
together.
Next to today's numbers,
stealing a few billion
rupiah.
almost looks small.
Put all of those
40 cases from 2005
on a chart,
and a wide gap
shows up between where
the corruption happened
most often and where
the big money went.
By case count,
state and regional budgets,
the APBN and APBD,
topped the list with
15 cases,
or 37.5% of the
total.
Yet those 15 regional
cases added up to
206.37 billion rubia,
or just 2.3%
of total state losses
that year.
The remaining 80.7%
came from banking and
finance,
where just seven cases
drained
7.13 trillion rubia.
P.S.
misused
two point zero two
trillion rupiah in BLBI
debit balance facilities.
Bank Mandiri issued Rp
1.89T in bad loans
to PT Kiani Kertas.
Fictitious letters of credit
drained 1.7 trillion from
Bank BNI Kebayoran Baru,
and another 1.3 trillion
vanishes from Bank Dagang
Bali.
Outside banking,
former ministers of religious
affairs,
embezzled
719 billion rupiah from
the Hajj and Ummah
Endowment funds.
Even the 2004 election
got dragged in,
with four separate KPU
cases totaling 15.53 billion
rupiah involving R.K.
over ballot paper,
N.S.
over poll worker Insurance,
D.D.
over election ink,
and M.W.K.,
who was caught bribing
a state auditor
with 300 million rupiah.
The difference between
2005 and the scandals
hitting Indonesia today
goes well beyond inflation.
For one,
the scale has exploded,
all 40 major cases
in 2005 combined for
8.84 trillion rupiah.
Whereas today,
a single case inside
a state-owned enterprise
or natural resource sector
routinely hits tens or
hundreds of trillions.
In the fuel import
and blending scandal at
state energy firm Pertamina
involving executive like
R.S.,
investigators calculated
annual state losses at
193.7 trillion rupiah.
The method have also
changed in 2005,
officials relied on procurement
markups on helicopters
or stationary and direct
cash skimming from local
budgets.
Today,
corrupt networks capture
entire supply chains,
manipulate import quotas
and rig licensing across
state monopolies.
Bribery has even reached
the courts themselves,
with recent cases catching
judges and court officials
taking tens of billions
rupiah just to fix
verdicts in major corporate
export cases.
Having anti-corruption
laws and special courts
on paper never stop
the theft.
Over the past 21
years,
corrupt actors adapted,
moving from skimming regional
budgets in the billions
to capturing state enterprises,
energy imports,
and judicial verdicts in
the trillions.
The loopholes in public
oversight
stayed open and the
penalties failed to scare
anyone off.
Between the brazen budget
skimming of 2005 and
the mega scandals inside
state enterprises today,
what do you think
went wrong with Indonesia's
fight against corruption?
